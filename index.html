The Vicious Cycle of Hunger

Hunger isn't caused by one thing, so this map shows the forces behind it and how they push on each other. At the centre is the UN's Sustainable Development Goal 2, Zero Hunger. Around it sit four other UN goals most closely tied to it: No Poverty and Clean Water decide how much hunger there is, and Good Health and Responsible Consumption feel its effects. Each goal holds five "agents", the forces and institutions my research found shaping hunger, such as armed conflict, food prices and school meals. The small coloured grid beside each agent shows how it affects every other one, from red (−2, makes hunger worse) to dark teal (+3, the two improve together).

What I found: five agents (armed conflict, extreme weather, food prices, fragile governments and unsafe water) keep making each other worse, so hunger keeps coming back even after help arrives. The tabs on the map walk through it. Vicious cycle shows how those five feed each other. Shock follows a single drought as it spreads through the whole system. Turning points proposes twelve practical responses, from solar cold rooms in food markets to drought insurance for farmers, each with the research showing where it has already worked.
